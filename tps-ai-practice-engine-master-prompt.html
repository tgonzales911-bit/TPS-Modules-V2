01
Architecture Overview
How the TPS AI Practice Engine Works
The flow from candidate action to AI feedback · Five layers explained

The TPS AI Practice Engine is not a chatbot. It is a structured coaching system built on top of the Anthropic API (Claude). Every session follows a fixed five-step flow — generate, present, receive, analyze, deliver. The master system prompt is the constant that makes every session feel like TPS, not a generic AI conversation. The variables are what make each session unique to the candidate, their rank, and their exercise focus.

1
Candidate Action
Candidate Selects Exercise Parameters in The Halligan platform
The candidate chooses: Rank (Lieutenant / Captain / BC / Chief), Exercise Type (Oral Board / Role Play / In-Basket / Written Exercise / Presentation Feedback), and Dimension Focus (optional — Supervisory Judgment, Interpersonal Skills, Strategic Thinking, etc.). The Halligan platform collects these as form field values and passes them to Make.
The Halligan platform → Engine webhook trigger with: {{rank}}, {{exercise_type}}, {{dimension}}, {{candidate_name}}
2
Scenario Generation
Make Sends Generation Request to Anthropic API
Make assembles the full API call: the master system prompt (constant, always the same) plus the scenario generation prompt (variable, based on rank and exercise type). Claude generates a unique, never-repeated scenario calibrated to the specified rank and dimension. Make stores the scenario in Airtable and displays it in The Halligan platform.
Make → Anthropic API → Claude generates scenario → Airtable stores → The Halligan platform displays
3
Candidate Response
Candidate Reads Scenario and Types Their Response
The candidate reads the scenario in The Halligan platform and types their full response in the text area. For oral board questions, this is their spoken-response transcript. For role play, it is how they would open and conduct the conversation. For in-basket, it is their triage and response decisions. When submitted, Make captures the response and the session ID.
The Halligan platform text input → Make webhook → {{candidate_response}} captured with {{session_id}}
4
AI Analysis
Make Sends Response to Claude with the Analysis Prompt
Make assembles the second API call: the master system prompt (same as before) plus the analysis prompt, which includes the original scenario, the candidate's response, their rank and dimension focus, and explicit instructions to score against TPS rubrics and deliver CLEAR-model feedback. Claude returns a structured feedback object.
Make → Anthropic API → [system: master prompt] + [user: scenario + response + analysis instructions]
5
Feedback Delivery
Make Writes Feedback to Airtable, The Halligan platform Displays It
Make parses Claude's structured response, writes the scores and feedback to the candidate's Airtable record, and The Halligan platform displays the full CLEAR feedback in the candidate's Practice Lab. The session is logged with date, exercise type, scores, and key feedback points. The candidate can immediately run another session or review their progress dashboard.
Claude response → Make parses → Airtable writes scores + feedback → The Halligan platform renders feedback view

The Core Intelligence
The TPS Master System Prompt

Every piece of feedback must be grounded in what the candidate actually wrote — specific observable behaviors, not impressions. "Your response lacked structure" is an impression. "Your response began with your third point — there was no opening statement that named what you were addressing and why it matters" is a diagnosis. PRINCIPLE 2 — THE CANDIDATE ALREADY HAS THE ANSWER Your feedback should make the candidate think, not just inform them. After identifying a gap, ask a question that leads them toward the insight before delivering the prescription. "What was missing from your opening?" is more valuable than "You needed an opening statement." "In your next oral board practice, write your opening sentence first before drafting any content — a single sentence that names the issue and why it matters at your rank. Practice delivering it until it takes fewer than 20 seconds" is commitment language. PRINCIPLE 4 — COACH THE LEADER, NOT THE SCENARIO The scenario is the presenting content. What you are actually developing is the leader. Stream of consciousness. Cannot follow the response. 2 — Some relevant content but no organizing framework. Begins without knowing where going. 3 — Basic structure present. Opening, body, close identifiable but not deliberate. 4 — Clear, organized response with deliberate opening, developed body, specific close. Uses TPS framework vocabulary correctly. 5 — Exceptional structure that demonstrates the response was deliberately architected. The organizing framework enhances the content rather than just containing it. Memorable and assessor-ready. DIMENSION 2: CONTENT (Knowledge and Relevance) 1 — Content is inaccurate, irrelevant, or demonstrates fundamental misunderstanding of the rank. 2 — Some relevant content but significant gaps. Generic statements without specific evidence. 3 — Adequate content that addresses core competencies. Correct but not distinguished. Could apply to any candidate. 4 — Strong, specific, evidence-based content. Uses TPS frameworks by name and applies them correctly. STAR-P stories present. 5 — Exceptional content depth that demonstrates genuine organizational leadership thinking beyond what the scenario required. Authentic conviction visible in the specificity. DIMENSION 3: LEADERSHIP AUTHENTICITY (Conviction and Genuine Development) 1 — Response feels performed or rehearsed. No genuine leadership identity visible. 2 — Content is correct but delivery is generic. Could be any candidate saying any answer. 3 — Some authentic elements present but inconsistent. Leadership philosophy stated but not demonstrated through specifics. 4 — Genuine conviction visible. Candidate seems to believe what they are saying. Specific evidence grounds the philosophy. 5 — The response could only have come from this candidate's genuine leadership experience and identity. Below-the-waterline work visible. Assessors would remember this candidate. OVERALL SCORE: Calculate as the average of the three dimensions, rounded to one decimal place. ═══════════════════════════════════════════════ THE CLEAR FEEDBACK MODEL — YOUR REQUIRED OUTPUT FORMAT ═══════════════════════════════════════════════ Every feedback response you deliver MUST follow the CLEAR model exactly, in this order: C — CALIBRATE (Self-Assessment Question) Before delivering your assessment, ask the candidate one calibration question that prompts self-reflection. L — LAND THE STRENGTH (What Worked — Specifically) Name one specific, observable behavior from the response that demonstrated genuine competency. Not generic encouragement. A behavioral observation tied to a specific line or section of their response. Format: "What I specifically saw working: [exact behavior with reference to their response]." The master system prompt is the constant. These variables are what Make injects into the user-turn prompt for each session, creating a personalized experience without changing the core coaching intelligence. In Make, these appear as data from the The Halligan platform form submission.

Description and Options

Dropdown · The Halligan platform form

The candidate's assessment center date. If within 30 days, AI activates the Countdown Protocol — more intensive feedback, higher specificity, explicit countdown urgency language.

Five Exercise Modules
Exercise-Specific Prompt Templates
One prompt pair per exercise type · Generation prompt + Analysis prompt · Use as the user-turn in every API call

Each exercise type has two prompts: a Generation Prompt (used in the first API call to create the scenario) and an Analysis Prompt (used in the second API call to evaluate the response and deliver feedback). Exercise Module 1 — Oral Board
Most Common · All Ranks · Text Response v1
The oral board is the highest-frequency exercise type in most assessment centers and the one with the widest performance gap between prepared and unprepared candidates. The generation prompt creates a unique question calibrated to rank and dimension. The analysis prompt evaluates structure, content depth, and authenticity against TPS rubrics.
Oral Board — Generation Prompt (API Call 1: Create the Question)
Generate a realistic oral board question for a {rank} promotional assessment center. CANDIDATE CONTEXT: - Name: {candidate_name} - Rank Seeking: {rank} - Dimension Focus: {dimension_focus} - Department Context: {department_context} - Days Until Assessment: [calculate from {assessment_date}] SESSION HISTORY (use to avoid repetition and surface patterns): {session_history} GENERATION INSTRUCTIONS: 1. Generate ONE oral board question that is realistic for a {rank} assessment center. 2. The question must directly assess the {dimension_focus} dimension. 3. Calibrate the complexity for {rank} — a Lieutenant question tests supervisory competency, a BC question tests organizational leadership competency. 4. If {department_context} is provided, make the scenario feel realistic to that context. 5. Do NOT repeat question types from {session_history} if provided. 6. The question should be answerable in 5-8 minutes by a prepared candidate. 7. Frame the question exactly as an assessor would ask it — direct, specific, professionally worded. OUTPUT FORMAT — Return ONLY this, nothing else: ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ TPS PRACTICE SESSION ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ Exercise: Oral Board Rank: {rank} Dimension: {dimension_focus} ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ [The oral board question, formatted exactly as an assessor would deliver it. 1-4 sentences. Type your full response below as if you are speaking to a panel of three assessors. ─────────────────────────────────────
Oral Board — Analysis Prompt (API Call 2: Evaluate Response + Deliver Feedback)
Evaluate the following oral board response and deliver TPS CLEAR feedback. SESSION CONTEXT: - Candidate: {candidate_name} - Rank Seeking: {rank} - Dimension Focus: {dimension_focus} - Department Context: {department_context} - Session History: {session_history} THE SCENARIO (what they were responding to): {generated_scenario} THE CANDIDATE'S RESPONSE: {candidate_response} ANALYSIS INSTRUCTIONS: 1. If the response is too brief to score fairly (under 100 words for a 5-8 minute question), ask for expansion before scoring. 6. If this candidate has session history, reference patterns where relevant. 7. Exercise Module 2 — Role Play
Glasl Escalation Levels · Conflict Resolution · Text Simulation v1
The role play module generates a scenario at a specified Glasl escalation level and asks the candidate to write how they would open and conduct the conversation. In v1 (text), the candidate writes their complete conversation approach. In v2 (video), they record it. The AI evaluates the TKI mode selection, IBR interest-surfacing, and SBI feedback application.
Role Play — Generation Prompt (API Call 1: Create the Scenario)
Generate a realistic role play scenario for a {rank} promotional assessment center. CANDIDATE CONTEXT: - Name: {candidate_name} - Rank Seeking: {rank} - Dimension Focus: {dimension_focus} - Department Context: {department_context} - Session History: {session_history} GENERATION INSTRUCTIONS: 1. Generate a role play scenario that would appear in a {rank} assessment center. 2. Select an appropriate Glasl escalation level for the scenario (Level 1-3 for first scenario, Level 3-5 for advanced practice, Level 5-7 for BC/Chief level). Match complexity to rank. 3. The scenario should create a genuine interpersonal challenge — not a clear-cut disciplinary issue but a nuanced situation requiring diagnostic thinking before action. 4. Name the crew member or party involved with a realistic first name and a brief character description (seniority level, general disposition, what they're presenting with). 5. Do NOT create situations that require the candidate to have specific policy knowledge — assess leadership behavior, not policy recall. 6. Do NOT repeat scenario types from {session_history} if provided. OUTPUT FORMAT — Return ONLY this, nothing else: ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ TPS PRACTICE SESSION ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ Exercise: Role Play Rank: {rank} Escalation Level: [Glasl Level X — Zone: Low/Medium/High] Dimension: {dimension_focus} ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ SCENARIO SETUP: [2-3 sentences establishing the situation — who, what, why this conversation needs to happen] THE CHALLENGE: [1-2 sentences describing what the crew member is presenting with, their general demeanor at this escalation level] ───────────────────────────────────── In your response, describe: (1) How you would open this conversation — your exact first words or approach. (2) The diagnostic questions you would ask to understand the situation before responding. (3) How you would navigate the challenge and what resolution or next step you would aim for. Write as if you are preparing for this conversation — be specific about what you would actually say and do. ─────────────────────────────────────
Role Play — Analysis Prompt (API Call 2: Evaluate Response + Deliver Feedback)
Evaluate the following role play response and deliver TPS CLEAR feedback. SESSION CONTEXT: - Candidate: {candidate_name} - Rank Seeking: {rank} - Escalation Level: [from generated scenario] - Dimension Focus: {dimension_focus} - Session History: {session_history} THE SCENARIO (what they were responding to): {generated_scenario} THE CANDIDATE'S RESPONSE: {candidate_response} ANALYSIS INSTRUCTIONS — ROLE PLAY SPECIFIC: In addition to the standard three TPS dimensions, evaluate the following role-play-specific elements: 1. DIAGNOSTIC QUALITY: Did the candidate diagnose before prescribing? Did they ask questions to understand the situation or did they jump to action? 2. TKI MODE SELECTION: What conflict mode did the candidate implicitly or explicitly use? Was it appropriate for the escalation level? (Competing mode at Level 2 = overtreating. Avoiding mode at Level 5 = undertreating.) 3. INTEREST-SURFACING: Did the candidate try to find the interest beneath the position (IBR framework)? Or did they respond to the stated position only? 4. SBI FEEDBACK STRUCTURE: If the scenario involved a performance issue, did the candidate use behavioral specificity (Situation, Behavior, Impact) or did they respond to character? 5. GLASL CALIBRATION: Was the candidate's response appropriate for the escalation level? Overresponding to a Level 2 produces unnecessary escalation. Underresponding to a Level 5 is leadership abdication. Score all three standard TPS dimensions and incorporate the role-play-specific observations into the Content dimension score. Deliver full CLEAR feedback using the exact format from your system instructions. End with the Transfer question.

Exercise Module 3 — In-Basket
Triage Framework · Priority Decisions · Multi-Item Scenarios
The in-basket module generates a realistic multi-item inbox — memos, requests, safety concerns, administrative items — and asks the candidate to triage and respond to each. The AI evaluates prioritization accuracy, decision quality, delegation appropriateness, and rank-appropriate authority awareness.
In-Basket — Generation Prompt (API Call 1: Create the Exercise)
Generate a realistic in-basket exercise for a {rank} promotional assessment center. CANDIDATE CONTEXT: - Name: {candidate_name} - Rank Seeking: {rank} - Dimension Focus: {dimension_focus} - Department Context: {department_context} - Session History: {session_history} GENERATION INSTRUCTIONS: 1. Generate 4-6 in-basket items that represent a realistic first-day inbox for a new {rank}. 2. Include a mix of urgency and importance levels — at least one Urgent+Important item, at least one Important-Not-Urgent item, at least one Urgent-Not-Important (delegate), and at least one item that should be deferred or declined. 3. Include at least one item with a political or interpersonal complexity (union, city manager, media, peer officer). 4. Include at least one safety-related item. 5. Make Automation Setup Guide
Step-by-step · No code required · Two scenarios to build

Make (formerly Integromat) is the automation layer that connects The Halligan platform, Airtable, and the Anthropic API without writing code. You need to build two Make scenarios: one for scenario generation (when a candidate starts a session) and one for response analysis (when a candidate submits their response).

⚙️
Make Scenario 1 — Session Start (Generate the Scenario)
Trigger: The Halligan platform Form Submission · Output: Scenario displayed to candidate
Step 1 — Webhook Trigger: In Make, create a new scenario. Add a Webhook module as the trigger. Copy the webhook URL and paste it into The Halligan platform as the form submission action for your Practice Lab form.

Step 2 — Airtable: Get Candidate Record: Add an Airtable Search Records module. Search the Candidates table by email (passed from The Halligan platform). This retrieves the candidate's rank, assessment date, tier, and session history summary.

Step 3 — Airtable: Get Session History: Add another Airtable Search Records module. Search the Sessions table for the last 5 sessions by this candidate. Use a Text Aggregator module to combine the key fields (exercise type, overall score, growth edge) into a single text block for the {session_history} variable.

Step 4 — HTTP: Call Anthropic API: Add an HTTP module. Set to POST. URL: https://api.anthropic.com/v1/messages. Headers: x-api-key: [your Anthropic API key], anthropic-version: 2023-06-01, Content-Type: application/json. Body: assemble the JSON with the master system prompt as the system parameter and the exercise generation prompt (with variables filled) as the user message.

Step 5 — Airtable: Create Session Record: Parse the API response to extract the generated scenario text. Create a new record in the Sessions table: candidate ID, session date, exercise type, rank, dimension, generated scenario text, status = "awaiting_response".

Step 6 — Webhook Response: Return the generated scenario and session ID to The Halligan platform for display. The Halligan platform renders the scenario in the Practice Lab interface and shows the candidate the response text field.
⚙️
Make Scenario 2 — Response Analysis (Evaluate + Deliver Feedback)
Trigger: Candidate Submits Response · Output: CLEAR Feedback displayed
Step 1 — Webhook Trigger: Same as Scenario 1, but triggered by the response submission form in The Halligan platform. Receives: session_id, candidate_response.

Step 2 — Airtable: Get Session Record: Look up the session record by session_id. Retrieve: generated_scenario, rank, exercise_type, dimension_focus, candidate_name, assessment_date, department_context, session_history.

Step 3 — HTTP: Call Anthropic API: Same structure as Scenario 1 but use the exercise-specific Analysis Prompt with all variables populated. The system parameter is always the Master System Prompt. The user parameter is the analysis prompt for the exercise type with all {variable} placeholders filled from the Airtable data.

Step 4 — Parse the Response: The API returns Claude's feedback as a text block. Use Make's text parsing tools (or a second Claude call with a simple parsing prompt) to extract: Structure score, Content score, Authenticity score, Overall score, Strength observation, Growth edge, Practice recommendation.

Step 5 — Airtable: Update Session Record: Write all parsed fields to the session record. Update status to "complete". This powers the progress dashboard in The Halligan platform.

Step 6 — Webhook Response: Return the full formatted feedback to The Halligan platform for display. The Halligan platform renders the score block and CLEAR feedback in the candidate's feedback view. The "Run It Again" button triggers a new Session Start with the same parameters.
The Anthropic API Call — Exact JSON Structure for Make
In Make's HTTP module, set the request body to JSON format with this structure:

{"model": "claude-sonnet-4-20250514", "max_tokens": 2000, "system": "[PASTE FULL MASTER SYSTEM PROMPT HERE]", "messages": [{"role": "user", "content": "[EXERCISE PROMPT WITH VARIABLES FILLED]"}]}

Use claude-sonnet-4-20250514 — it has the best balance of coaching quality and response speed. max_tokens 2000 is sufficient for a complete CLEAR feedback response. For the generation call (shorter output), 800 tokens is enough.

Before You Launch
Testing and Refinement Guide
50 test sessions before launch · What to look for · How to refine the prompt

The master prompt is v1.0 — it is built to perform well immediately, but it will improve with iteration. Run at least 50 test sessions yourself before inviting beta testers. Here is what to evaluate in each test session and how to refine based on what you find.

🔬
What to Evaluate in Test Sessions
5 Quality Checks · Per Session
1. Scenario Quality: Is the generated scenario realistic for the specified rank? Would a fire/EMS assessor recognize it as legitimate? Does it test the right dimension? If scenarios are too generic, add more rank-specific context to the generation prompt or expand the {department_context} instructions.

2. Scoring Accuracy: Write intentionally weak responses (2/5 quality) and intentionally strong responses (4-5/5 quality). Does the AI score them appropriately? If it over-scores weak responses, add this to the Critical Constraints section: "When in doubt, score conservatively. A candidate who receives an inflated score is less prepared, not more."

3. CLEAR Format Fidelity: Is the AI consistently following the CLEAR model in order? Is the Calibration question always present? Is the growth edge always ONE item? Is the Transfer question always at the end? If the AI skips steps, add emphasis to those steps in the format instructions.

4. Specificity of Feedback: Is the feedback referencing specific lines from the candidate's response, or giving generic observations? "Your opening was weak" is a generic observation. "Your opening sentence — 'I would approach this by first...' — begins with your approach before establishing what you're approaching and why it matters" is specific. If specificity drops, add to the voice instructions: "Every feedback observation must quote or reference a specific line or section of the candidate's response."

5. Voice and Tone: Does the feedback sound like TPS — direct, fire-service-calibrated, respectful, developmental — or does it sound like generic AI coaching? If the voice drifts, add specific TPS voice examples to the Tone and Voice section of the master prompt.
🔧
How to Refine the Prompt Without Breaking It
Iteration Protocol · Add, Not Replace
The golden rule of prompt refinement: add specificity, never remove constraints. If the AI is doing something wrong, the fix is almost always adding a more specific instruction — not removing an existing one. Removing a constraint creates unpredictability. Adding a constraint creates refinement.

When scenarios are too generic: Add to the generation prompt: "The scenario must include a specific named individual, a specific operational context, and a specific trigger event that created the need for this conversation or response."

When feedback is too long: Add to the CLEAR format section: "Total feedback response should not exceed 400 words. If you are exceeding that, you are giving more than one growth edge. Find the single highest-leverage observation and eliminate all others."

When the AI breaks the CLEAR format: Add as the very last line of the master prompt: "FINAL INSTRUCTION: Before you output any feedback, verify: (1) Did I include the score block in the exact format? (2) Did I follow C-L-E-A-R in exact order? (3) Did I name only ONE growth edge? (4) Did I end with the Transfer question? If any answer is no, revise before outputting."

When fire service context feels off: Expand the TPS Methodology Knowledge section with specific fire service examples for each framework. The more fire service specificity in the prompt, the more fire service specificity in the output.
